Germany got Hobbes early, through *De Cive* and the Latin *Leviathan* rather
than the English, and read him as a systematiser rather than a scandal. That
difference lasted. Where the English argued about whether he was an atheist,
the Germans argued about whether the state he described was the only one
possible.

### Natural law takes the method

Samuel von Pufendorf is the pivot. He took the ambition — a science of morals
and politics deduced from a description of human nature — and rejected the
description, putting weakness and need where Hobbes had put aggression and
pride, and deriving sociability where Hobbes had derived war. The result was
the textbook natural law of the next century, taught in every Protestant
university in Europe, and it carried Hobbes's structure into places that would
never have read Hobbes.

Leibniz read him with more care and less patience. His objection is the
sharpest one made against Hobbes in that century: a doctrine in which justice
is constituted by will makes the justice of God an empty compliment, and the
same argument, run downwards, makes the justice of a sovereign no reason to
obey rather than merely a fact about who is stronger.

### Kant and Hegel

Kant devoted the second half of a 1793 essay to answering Hobbes by name. He
concedes the conclusion Hobbes cared about — there is no right of rebellion —
and refuses the premise that gets there, that the subject has surrendered
everything: a people has rights against its ruler that it cannot give away, and
a freedom of the pen through which it may say so. Obedience without submission
is the compromise, and most of liberal constitutionalism has been trying to
make it work ever since.

Hegel does not argue with Hobbes so much as rewrite him. The struggle for
recognition in the *Phenomenology* is the state of nature with the object
changed: two self-consciousnesses fight to the death not over scarce goods or
security but over acknowledgement, which is much closer to Hobbes's
[glory](/concepts/honour/) than to his competition. That the fight ends in
mastery and servitude rather than in a covenant is the whole difference between
the two traditions.

### Tönnies, and the invention of the field

Modern Hobbes scholarship is German, and it starts with Ferdinand Tönnies.
He went into the British Museum, found the manuscripts of *The Elements of
Law* and *Behemoth*, edited both, and then wrote the first life and doctrine
built on the complete works rather than on *Leviathan* alone. Every subsequent
edition rests on that work, including the
[Clarendon](/further-reading/#malcolm-leviathan).

It was not antiquarianism. Tönnies's own *Gemeinschaft und Gesellschaft* (1887)
divides social life into community, which grows, and association, which is
made by agreement between individuals pursuing their own ends — and names
Hobbes as the theorist of the second. German sociology's founding distinction
has *Leviathan* on one side of it.

### Weimar, and the argument about modernity

Then the reception turned into a fight about what the modern state was for.

[Carl Schmitt](/readings/totalitarian/) had taken *auctoritas non veritas facit
legem* from the Latin *Leviathan* as the motto of *Politische Theologie* in
1922, and returned in 1938 with a book that reads the frontispiece as an
emblem of failure: by allowing subjects a private inward faith while requiring
outward conformity, Hobbes admitted the distinction that liberalism would later
use to hollow the state out. The book is inseparable from the fact that Schmitt
had joined the Nazi party in 1933 and was writing inside the regime.

[Leo Strauss](/further-reading/#strauss-political-philosophy) wrote his study
in exile in England in 1936, having already produced a set of notes on
Schmitt's *Concept of the Political* in 1932, and reached the opposite verdict
by a similar route: the system rests on a moral attitude to violent death
rather than on the new science, so Hobbes is the founder of modernity and
modernity is a lowering of the aims of political life.

Reinhart Koselleck's *Kritik und Krise* completes the sequence after the war.
The conscience Hobbes made private, on condition that it kept quiet, did not
stay quiet: it became a moral tribunal that judged the state from outside, and
the Enlightenment is the record of that judgement. Habermas argued with the
book, which is one reason it is still argued with.

### After

Postwar German scholarship — Bernard Willms, Wolfgang Kersting, Reinhart
Brandt, and the cooperative commentaries that German academic culture produces
so well — turned the fight back into a field. But the extreme positions are
still the German ones. Nobody in England has claimed that Hobbes founded
modernity, and nobody in America has claimed that he destroyed it.
