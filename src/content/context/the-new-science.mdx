*Leviathan* announces its method before it names its subject. The first
sentence of the Introduction treats nature as God's machinery and the state
as man's imitation of it:

> Nature (the art whereby God hath made and governes the world) is by the art
> of man, as in many other things, so in this also imitated, that it can make
> an Artificial Animal. For seeing life is but a motion of Limbs, the begining
> whereof is in some principall part within; why may we not say, that all
> Automata (Engines that move themselves by springs and wheeles as doth a
> watch) have an artificiall life? For what is the Heart, but a Spring; and
> the Nerves, but so many Strings; and the Joynts, but so many Wheeles?
>
> — [The Introduction](/chapters/the-introduction/)

No earlier political treatise begins like this, because no earlier political
theorist believed it. The sentence assumes a revolution in natural philosophy
that was still being fought when Hobbes wrote, and *Leviathan* is, among
everything else, an attempt to annex politics to the winning side.

### Geometry

The revolution reached Hobbes through a book left open on a table. He had
taken a thorough humanist education at Oxford — logic and Aristotelian
physics, which he later said taught him nothing — and was a middle-aged
gentleman's secretary when, at forty, he came upon Euclid's *Elements* open
at the forty-seventh proposition, read it, declared it impossible, and
followed the chain of demonstrations back to the axioms until he was
convinced. What seized him was not the theorem but rather the kind of
assent it compelled: conclusions that no honest reader could dispute,
because every term had been defined and every step shown. Nothing in the
moral or political learning of Europe worked like that, and Hobbes drew the
conclusion that this was why Europe's moral and political learning had ended
in war. *Leviathan* keeps geometry as its standard of what knowing is:

> Geometry, (which is the onely Science that it hath pleased God hitherto to
> bestow on mankind)
>
> — [Chapter 4](/chapters/04-of-speech/)

The parenthesis carries the programme. Geometry is the only science *so
far*; the book in the reader's hands is the application for the second
place.

### Galileo

The other conversion was to motion. The century after Copernicus's *De
revolutionibus* (*On the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres*, 1543) had
slowly dismantled the Aristotelian cosmos, and
Galileo finished the demolition: the telescope in 1610, the *Dialogue* in
1632, and beneath the astronomy a new principle — that motion, not rest, is
the natural state of bodies, and that a thing once moving continues until
something stops it. Aristotle's world, where everything seeks its place and
comes to rest in it, was exactly wrong. Hobbes met the new doctrine on his
third continental tour, in the <Gloss note="Galileo was condemned by the
Roman Inquisition in June 1633 for defending the motion of the earth, and
lived his last years under house arrest at his villa at Arcetri, outside
Florence, where Hobbes — by his own and Aubrey's account — visited him in
1636.">year of Galileo's house arrest</Gloss>, visiting the condemned man at
Arcetri in 1636; he wrote later, dedicating [*De Corpore*](/works/de-corpore/),
that Galileo was the first to open to us the gate of natural philosophy
universal, which is the knowledge of the nature of motion.

Hobbes's originality was to drive the principle inward. If everything is
body, and every change is motion, then sensation, imagination, and passion
are motions too, and a science of man can be built the way Galileo built a
science of falling bodies. The famous inversions of Part I are Galilean
physics applied to the soul. Sense is not the reception of a form but rather
a collision — "the cause of Sense, is the Externall Body, or Object, which
presseth the organ proper to each Sense" ([Chapter 1](/chapters/01-of-sense/))
— and [imagination](/concepts/imagination/) is inertia in the head:

> Imagination therefore is nothing but Decaying Sense; and is found in men,
> and many other living Creatures, as well sleeping, as waking.
>
> — [Chapter 2](/chapters/02-of-imagination/)

Between sense and action Hobbes inserts [endeavour](/concepts/endeavour/),
motion too small to see, so that desire and fear become physics as well; and
William Harvey — a friend, whose *De Motu Cordis* (*On the Motion of the
Heart*, 1628) had shown the blood
itself to be in perpetual circulation — supplied the demonstration that the
body is a hydraulic engine. The Introduction's spring, strings, and wheels
are not a metaphor. They are the theory.

### Paris

The workshop of all this was Paris, and its foreman was the friar
Marin Mersenne, whose cell served the mathematicians of Europe as a post office.
Hobbes entered the circle in the 1630s and belonged to it for his eleven
years of [exile](/hobbes/life/): he wrote the Third Objections to Descartes's
*Meditations* — the two men disputing, with mounting ill temper, which of
them had mechanised the mind first and better — and found a durable ally in
[Pierre Gassendi](/kindred/gassendi/), who was performing the same operation
on Epicurus. In his youth Hobbes had taken dictation from Francis Bacon on
the walks at Gorhambury, and something of Bacon's programme — knowledge as
power over nature, for the relief of man's estate — survives in him; but the
method he trusted was never Bacon's patient induction. It was definition and
deduction, reasoning as computation:

> Reason, in this sense, is nothing but Reckoning (that is, Adding and
> Substracting) of the Consequences of generall names agreed upon, for the
> Marking and Signifying of our thoughts
>
> — [Chapter 5](/chapters/05-of-reason-and-science/)

His grand design was a trilogy deriving everything in order — body, man,
citizen — but the [war](/context/the-english-civil-war/) inverted it: the
political volume, [*De Cive*](/works/de-cive/), was published first, in 1642,
because (as he said in its preface) it had been ripened and plucked before
its time. *Leviathan* repeats the wager at full length and in English. The
state of nature itself is a piece of method before it is a piece of
anthropology: take the commonwealth apart in thought, as a watchmaker takes
apart a watch, and see what the pieces do when nothing holds them — which is
why [Chapter 9](/chapters/09-of-the-severall-subjects-of-knowledge/) can
table politics among the sciences, a body of consequences as demonstrable as
any, since we ourselves make the definitions on which it rests.

### The old science

The polemical shadow of all this falls across Part IV. What the new science
displaced was not ignorance but rather a rival system — Aristotle as
digested by the Schools, with its separated essences, substantial forms, and
qualities that explain heaviness by a desire to be low. Hobbes's verdict is
total:

> scarce any thing can be more absurdly said in naturall Philosophy, than
> that which now is called Aristotles Metaphysiques
>
> — [Chapter 46](/chapters/46-of-darknesse-from-vain-philosophy-and-fabulous-traditions/)

And the polemic is political, because the absurdities were load-bearing. A
philosophy of incorporeal substances underwrites a clergy that commands
souls; the doctrine of essences walking without bodies props up the
[Kingdome of Darknesse](/concepts/kingdom-of-darkness/); the
[universities](/chapters/a-review-and-conclusion/) that taught
"Aristotelity" trained the preachers who taught rebellion. For Hobbes the
new science and the new politics were one campaign against one enemy, which
is why *Leviathan* needs both. The old alliance it broke — kings holding
their crowns immediately from God, with no geometry required — is
[the next page](/context/divine-right-and-absolutism/).
