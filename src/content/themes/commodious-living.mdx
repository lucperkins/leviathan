The reputation of *Leviathan* is that it is about death. It is, but the
famous sentence of Chapter XIII is a list of absences, and reading what is
absent tells you what the book is for:

> no Culture of the Earth; no Navigation, nor use of the commodities that may
> be imported by Sea; no commodious Building; no Instruments of moving, and
> removing such things as require much force; no Knowledge of the face of the
> Earth; no account of Time; no Arts; no Letters; no Society

Only then does the sentence arrive at continual fear and danger of violent
death, and the life of man, solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short. The
horror of the state of nature is not only that it kills you. It is that
nothing can be built in it.

### One of three passions

Hobbes says as much a few paragraphs later, and the sentence is easy to read
past because the famous one is next to it:

> The Passions that encline men to Peace, are Feare of Death; Desire of such
> things as are necessary to commodious living; and a Hope by their Industry
> to obtain them.

Three passions, not one. Fear does the pushing, but the wanting of a decent
life does as much of the work, and hope of getting it by one's own effort does
the rest. A politics built on fear alone would explain why men submit; it
would not explain why they would bother to stay.

### The sovereign owes it

The point returns as a duty in
[Chapter 30](/chapters/30-of-the-office-of-the-soveraign-representative/),
where the office of the sovereign is the safety of the people, and safety is
immediately defined outward:

> But by Safety here, is not meant a bare Preservation, but also all other
> Contentments of life, which every man by lawfull Industry, without danger,
> or hurt to the Common-wealth, shall acquire to himselfe.

What follows is the least absolutist page in the book: equal taxes, public
charity for those who cannot work, the prevention of idleness, good laws
meaning needful ones, and instruction so that subjects understand the
arrangement they live under.
[Chapter 24](/chapters/24-of-the-nutrition-and-procreation-of-a-common-wealth/)
adds the commercial machinery — property, trade, money as the blood of the
commonwealth — as part of the same office.

### Why it changes the picture

Read without this, Hobbes is a theorist of terror whose state exists to stop
men killing each other. Read with it, the state exists so that there can be
agriculture, shipping, buildings, clocks, arts, letters and company, and
stopping men killing each other is the precondition rather than the purpose.
The two readings license quite different politics, and the second is closer to
what the text says — which is part of why
[C. B. Macpherson](/further-reading/#macpherson-possessive-individualism)
could find a market society inside the state of nature, and why the
[claim on him made by economics](/ancestor/) is not far-fetched.
