Before any argument begins, the Introduction sets the terms in a single
sentence:

> Nature (the art whereby God hath made and governes the world) is by the art
> of man, as in many other things, so in this also imitated, that it can make
> an Artificial Animal.
>
> — Introduction

Everything follows from taking that literally. God's making of the world is
called an art; man's making of the commonwealth is the same kind of activity
at a lower level. Watches have artificial life because a spring is a heart and
wheels are joints. The state is the next thing up: a body built out of bodies.

### The anatomy

Hobbes then does something no political writer had done. He goes through the
artificial man part by part and names the natural organ each one imitates: the
sovereignty is an artificial soul, giving life and motion to the whole; the
magistrates are artificial joints; reward and punishment are the nerves;
wealth is strength; counsellors the memory; equity and laws an artificial
reason and will; concord is health, sedition sickness, and civil war death.
The [frontispiece](/) is the same list drawn.

It is a conceit, and it is also a method. If the commonwealth is made, then it
can be taken apart and explained by how it was assembled, which is exactly
what the rest of the book does. To know a thing is to know how it is
generated, and a thing you generated yourself you can know completely.

### Why it has to be artificial

The alternative was on the shelf and Hobbes refuses it. Aristotle held that
man is a political animal and the city a natural growth, and the evidence was
that [bees and ants](/themes/animals/) live sociably without a coercive power.
Hobbes concedes the insects and takes the conclusion away: their agreement is
natural, ours is by covenant, and that is precisely why ours needs a common
power and theirs does not. Political order is not something we grow into. It
is a thing we manufacture, badly and at cost, because nothing in us produces
it on its own.

### The mechanism

Two pieces of engineering make it possible.

The first is [personation](/concepts/person/), in
[Chapter 16](/chapters/16-of-persons-authors-and-things-personated/). A person
may be natural, his words his own, or artificial, his words owned by somebody
else. Once representation is available a multitude can be made into one
person, and the commonwealth is what you get when every one of them authorises
the same representative. Without this chapter the covenant of
[Chapter 17](/chapters/17-of-the-causes-generation-and-definition-of-a-common-wealth/)
could not be described at all, which is why the [*Elements of Law*](/works/elements-of-law/) and
[*De Cive*](/works/de-cive/), written before Hobbes had the doctrine, are weaker at exactly this
point.

The second is law, and the image is the best in the book:

> But as men, for the atteyning of peace, and conservation of themselves
> thereby, have made an Artificiall Man, which we call a Common-wealth; so
> also have they made Artificiall Chains, called Civill Lawes, which they
> themselves, by mutuall covenants, have fastned at one end, to the lips of
> that Man, or Assembly, to whom they have given the Soveraigne Power; and at
> the other end to their own Ears.

The chains are of our own making, we attached both ends ourselves, and the
sentence that follows admits they are weak: they hold by the danger of
breaking them rather than by the difficulty.

### Artificial eternity

The last piece is in
[Chapter 19](/chapters/19-of-the-severall-kinds-of-common-wealth-by-institution/),
and it is easy to read past. The matter of the commonwealth is mortal: kings
die, and so do assemblies. So the artificer needs a device for making the
made thing outlast the material, and the device is the right of succession —
which Hobbes, entirely deadpan, calls an artificial eternity. A mortal god is
kept alive by a rule about who speaks next.

### What it costs

The word cuts both ways, and Hobbes knows it. An artificial thing is a made
thing, so it can be made badly, and Part IV is the account of what bad
workmanship looks like: names never defined, essences separated from bodies,
a spiritual power built beside the civil one. The
[kingdom of darkness](/concepts/kingdom-of-darkness/) is not a rival kingdom
so much as a botched artifice.

And a made thing can be unmade. The same paragraph that gives the
commonwealth its artificial soul ends with civil war as its death. Nothing
guarantees the artificial man; he was assembled by frightened people out of
nothing but each other, and he lasts exactly as long as
[the power lasteth](/chapters/21-of-the-liberty-of-subjects/) by which he
protects them.
