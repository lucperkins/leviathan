The third law of nature is that men perform their covenants made, and
Chapter XV takes the definition straight from it:

> But when a Covenant is made, then to break it is Unjust: And the definition
> of INJUSTICE, is no other than The Not Performance Of Covenant.

Everything else follows negatively. Whatsoever is not unjust is just.

### Nothing is unjust before a covenant

Chapter XIII had already said it: where there is no common power there is no
law, and where no law, no injustice. Force and fraud are the two cardinal
virtues of war, and the notions of right and wrong have no place there — not
because men in that condition are wicked, but because the words have nothing
to attach to. Justice is not a quality of a soul or an order in the world. It
is a relation to an agreement, and agreements need a power over them.

### The Foole

Hobbes then answers the hardest objection to his own position, and gives it
the best lines in the chapter. The Foole says in his heart there is no such
thing as justice: since every man's preservation is committed to his own
care, breaking a covenant when it pays must be reasonable. Hobbes's reply is
not that this is wicked but that it is a miscalculation. A man who breaks
faith cannot be received into any society except by an error of those who
receive him, and cannot rely on that error; and he who thinks it reasonable
to deceive those that help him cannot reasonably expect any other means of
safety than his own force. The reasoning is prudential all the way down, which
is either the strength of the argument or its weakness, depending on the
reader.

### Commutative and distributive

The chapter also disposes of the classical division. Aristotle's commutative
justice, an equality of value in exchange, Hobbes reduces to the keeping of
contracts, since the value of a thing is what the parties are content to give.
Distributive justice he redefines as the justice of an arbitrator — equity,
distributing to each his own — which is the eleventh law of nature and not a
principle for dividing up society at all.
