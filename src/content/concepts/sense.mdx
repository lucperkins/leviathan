For Hobbes, *sense* is where everything begins. Every thought a person has is
either a sensation or something derived from one:

> The Originall of them all, is that which we call Sense; (For there is no
> conception in a mans mind, which hath not at first, totally, or by parts,
> been begotten upon the organs of Sense.)

### A mechanical account

Sense is not a mysterious faculty but rather a chain of motions. An external body
presses on a sense organ, directly (taste, touch) or through a medium
(sight, hearing, smell). That pressure is carried by nerves to the brain and
heart, where it meets a counter-pressure, an *endeavour* of the body to push
back. Because that endeavour is directed outward, what we experience seems to
be located outside us. This "seeming" is what Hobbes calls *fancy*, and fancy
is what sense actually consists of.

### Qualities are in us, not in objects

The colours, sounds, and smells we perceive are not properties of the objects
that cause them. They are motions in us, produced by motions in matter. Hobbes
argues from reflection and echo: the object is in one place, the appearance in
another, so the appearance cannot be *in* the object. This is an early and
influential statement of the distinction later called primary versus secondary
qualities.

### Against the schools

Hobbes closes the chapter by attacking the Aristotelian doctrine of *species*
taught in the universities, the idea that objects send out "visible species"
or "intelligible species" that are received by the eye or understanding. He
treats this as an example of *insignificant speech*: words that name nothing.
The remark foreshadows a running theme of *Leviathan*, that confused language
is a political danger, not just a philosophical one.
