Everything that happens in the book is a body moving. Hobbes takes from
Galileo the principle the old philosophy had lacked, and states it with the
sea in it:

> When a Body is once in motion, it moveth (unless something els hinder it)
> eternally; and whatsoever hindreth it, cannot in an instant, but in time,
> and by degrees quite extinguish it: And as wee see in the water, though the
> wind cease, the waves give not over rowling for a long time after

The Schools had taught the opposite: that motion is a thing to be explained
and rest the state a body seeks, each seeking its own place. Once the
principle is turned around, rest needs no explaining and appetite no apology.
A moving thing is not on its way to stillness but rather continuing as it
must, and the book's account of man is built on nothing else.

### Sense is motion

Chapter I opens the system by reducing the felt world to pressure. The
qualities we call sensible are "so many several motions of the matter, by
which it presseth our organs diversly." Colour, sound, heat are in us, and in
us they are motion too; the object has nothing but its own.
[Imagination](/concepts/imagination/) is the same motion outlasting its cause
— decaying [sense](/concepts/sense/), the waves still rolling after the wind
— and a train of thought is one motion handing on to the next.

### Two motions in a man

Chapter VI divides the motions of animals in two. Vital motion — the course
of the blood, the pulse, breathing — begins in generation and never asks the
imagination's leave. Voluntary motion — going, speaking, striking — is first
fancied in the mind, and its invisible beginnings are
[endeavour](/concepts/endeavour/): toward what causes it,
[appetite](/concepts/desire/); away, aversion. By this one step the passions
become physics, and the geometry that handles bodies can be asked to handle
men.

### Nothing rests

A creature made of motions has no stopping place. There is no finall aim or
greatest good; [felicity](/concepts/felicity/) is "a continuall progresse of
the desire, from one object to another," and the general inclination of
mankind is "a perpetuall and restlesse desire of Power after power, that
ceaseth onely in Death." Politics inherits the physics whole. The
Introduction offers the [Common-wealth](/concepts/commonwealth/) as an
artificial man whose sovereignty is "an Artificiall Soul, as giving life and
motion to the whole body"; civil war is that motion failing, which the book
calls death. Even the state, [nature](/concepts/nature/) imitated by art,
keeps still only by moving.
