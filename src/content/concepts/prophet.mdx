> The name of PROPHET, signifieth in Scripture sometimes Prolocutor; that is,
> he that speaketh from God to Man, or from man to God: And sometimes
> Praedictor, or a foreteller of things to come; And sometimes one that
> speaketh incoherently, as men that are distracted.

The third sense is not a joke. Hobbes cites the cases and means them: the word
covers ecstatic speech, and a reader who assumes it always means the first
sense will get the scripture wrong.

### How God speaks

Chapter XXXVI works through the ways God is said to have spoken to men —
dreams, visions, voices, and by the mouth of another prophet — and finds that
every one of them, described honestly, is something happening in a man's
imagination. To say that God spoke to me in a dream is no more than to say I
dreamed that God spoke to me. Hobbes does not deny that God can do it; he
denies that anyone else has a way of checking.

### The two marks

So the question becomes practical, and the answer is deliberately hard to
satisfy: a true prophet shows two marks together, the doing of miracles and
the teaching of no other religion than that already established. Either alone
is insufficient. Since [miracles](/concepts/miracle/) have ceased, the first
mark is unavailable, and what remains is conformity with the doctrine the
commonwealth already holds.

That is a striking conclusion for a book that spends its second half on
scripture: private revelation can never be evidence to anybody but the person
who has it, and a claim to prophecy is therefore a claim to authority that
cannot be tested. Chapter XXIX lists exactly that — the pretence of
inspiration — among the
[diseases of a commonwealth](/themes/diseases-of-the-commonwealth/).
