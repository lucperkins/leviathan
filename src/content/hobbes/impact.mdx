Almost nobody agreed with him. *Leviathan* pleased neither side of the war it
was written about: royalists read the Review and Conclusion as an argument for
submitting to Cromwell, parliamentarians read the rest as an argument for
absolute monarchy, and the clergy of every description read Parts III and IV
and concluded that the author did not believe in an immortal soul. The book
made him famous in the way a scandal makes a man famous, and it stayed that
way for two hundred years.

### The charge of atheism

The danger became concrete in October 1666. After the plague and the fire, a
Commons committee was empowered to receive information about books tending to
atheism and profaneness, and it was instructed to look in particular at
*Leviathan*. Nothing came of the bill, but Hobbes, then seventy-eight, burned
papers, read up on the law of heresy, and wrote an appendix to the Latin
edition arguing that no court in England had jurisdiction to try him for it.
That Latin edition appeared in Amsterdam in 1668, because he could no longer
print on such subjects at home: the King protected him with a pension and a
private welcome, and forbade him to publish on religion or politics.
[*Behemoth*](/works/behemoth/), his history of the civil war, was refused a licence and appeared
only after his death.

The University of Oxford finished the job four years after he died. Its
*Judgment and Decree* of 21 July 1683 condemned a list of propositions drawn
from *Leviathan* and [*De Cive*](/works/de-cive/), and the books were burned in the Schools
quadrangle. The doctrine that self-preservation is the foundation of civil
society was the first proposition on the list.

### Hobbism

He had made his own answerers. [Bishop Bramhall](/hobbes/faith/), who had
argued with him about free will for twenty years, published *The Catching of
Leviathan* in 1658; Edward Hyde, Earl of Clarendon, who had known him at the
exiled court, wrote a book-length *Brief View and Survey* of the errors of
*Leviathan* in 1676; [John Wallis](/hobbes/career/) attacked the mathematics
and the politics together; Ralph Cudworth and the Cambridge Platonists built
much of their philosophy as a reply to him. The epithets stuck faster than the
arguments. *Hobbism* meant selfishness dressed as reason, *the monster of
Malmesbury* meant Hobbes, and both were current long after anyone could say
what was in the book.

### What the next century took

The people who used him mostly did not say so. Spinoza had *De Cive* and drew
on it in the *Tractatus Theologico-Politicus* (*Theological-Political
Treatise*) of 1670, while marking the exact
point of difference: he preserves natural right entire, and Hobbes gives it
up. Pufendorf built the standard European textbook of natural law partly out
of Hobbes and partly against him. Locke, whose *Second Treatise* answers the
Hobbesian position at length, does not name him in it. Rousseau names him and
attacks him, and takes his starting point anyway.

What survived was the method rather than the conclusion: begin with
individuals who have no political ties, ask what they would agree to, and
derive the state's authority from the answer. Nearly everyone who used it
rejected the absolute sovereign it was built to justify. That tradition, and
the way it split, is the subject of the page on
[contractarianism](/themes/contractarianism/).

### Eclipse and recovery

For most of the nineteenth century he was a name in a footnote, and an
embarrassing one. The collected edition of Sir William Molesworth, published
between 1839 and 1845, made the texts available; it took another fifty years
for anyone to build on them.
Ferdinand Tönnies, working in the manuscripts, printed *Behemoth* and the
[*Elements of Law*](/works/elements-of-law/) for the first time from Hobbes's own copies in 1889 and
wrote the first modern life in 1896. The twentieth century then produced a
Hobbes for every position: Leo Strauss in 1936 arguing that the whole system
rests on a moral attitude to death rather than on the new science;
Carl Schmitt in 1938 reading the frontispiece as the tragedy of a state
that conceded too much to private belief; Michael Oakeshott in 1946 presenting
him as the greatest and most sceptical of English philosophers; C. B. Macpherson
in 1962 finding a seventeenth century market society hidden inside the state
of nature.

### How he is read now

The standing disagreements are worth knowing before reading the secondary
literature. Whether Hobbes is a consistent egoist or a natural law theorist
whose laws bind because God commands them was the question A. E. Taylor and
Howard Warrender put in the middle of the subject in 1938 and 1957, and it has
never been settled. Whether the religion of Parts III and IV is sincere or
tactical divides readers the same way it divided his contemporaries, and is
taken up on the page about [his faith](/hobbes/faith/). Quentin Skinner has
spent a career arguing that the questions themselves are wrong unless the book
is put back among the arguments it was written into. Meanwhile the state of
nature has been rewritten as a problem in game theory, and the covenant as its
solution, by David Gauthier and Jean Hampton among others; Hobbes turns out to
have described a collective action problem with some precision three centuries
before there was a name for one.

### The word itself

The most durable thing he made was a picture. *Leviathan* is now the ordinary
word for the modern state, used by people who have never opened the book, and
the frontispiece is the standard illustration of what a state is. The
international relations literature borrowed a sentence from Chapter XIII and
built a school on it:

> But though there had never been any time, wherein particular men were in a
> condition of warre one against another; yet in all times, Kings, and persons
> of Soveraigne authority, because of their Independency, are in continuall
> jealousies, and in the state and posture of Gladiators.

Anarchy between states, order within them, and no world sovereign to make the
difference: that is the frame, and *Hobbesian* is what it is called, whether
or not Hobbes would have recognised the use. The argument about where the
state comes from and what makes it fail is still conducted in his vocabulary,
and still with his image at the front of it.
