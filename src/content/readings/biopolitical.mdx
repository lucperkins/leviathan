Everyone remembers that the subjects lay down their right of nature. Almost
nobody notices what Hobbes says about the person they lay it down to.

### The sentence

[Chapter 28](/chapters/28-of-punishments-and-rewards/) opens with a question
Hobbes calls one of much importance: by what door did the right of punishing
come in? It cannot have been given by the subjects, because no man is bound by
covenant not to resist violence, and nobody had a right to lay hands on his
neighbour to transfer in the first place. So where did it come from?

> For the Subjects did not give the Soveraign that right; but onely in laying
> down theirs, strengthned him to use his own, as he should think fit, for the
> preservation of them all: so that it was not given, but left to him, and to
> him onely; and (excepting the limits set him by naturall Law) as entire, as
> in the condition of meer Nature, and of warre of every one against his
> neighbour.

The covenant does not create the sovereign's power. It clears a space around
it. Everybody else steps out of the state of nature and one party is left
standing in it, holding the right of nature entire, in the middle of the
commonwealth. The war of all against all has not been ended. It has been
reduced to one participant.

Hobbes puts this forward as a technical solution to a technical difficulty,
and moves on within a paragraph. It is the sentence the whole of this reading
is built on.

### Foucault, and the head of the king

Michel Foucault came at it from the other direction. His argument in the 1976
lectures is that political theory has never managed to stop thinking in terms
of sovereignty — of a power that says no, that takes, that decides who dies —
long after the actual mechanics of power stopped working that way. What
replaced it he called biopower: a power that administers life, measures
populations, encourages some kinds of living and quietly abandons others. The
old formula was to make die and let live. The new one is to make live and let
die.

Hobbes is the obstacle in that story rather than an ally. Foucault's more
specific charge is that the state of nature is a piece of theatre: not a real
war but a stage of representations, of postures and calculations of who might
attack, invented to displace a rival account then circulating in England — the
discourse of conquest, of Normans and Saxons, of a society permanently split by
a war that never stopped. On this account Hobbes did not discover that politics
rests on war. He invented a fictional war in order to get the real one out of
the way.

### Agamben, and the wolf

Giorgio Agamben went back to the text and found Chapter 28 waiting. If the
sovereign retains the right of nature, then the state of nature is not
something the commonwealth left behind in prehistory; it is a zone the
sovereign carries inside himself and can open at any point. That is the state
of exception: law suspended by law, and a person left exposed to violence that
is neither punishment nor crime.

Hobbes supplies the figure as well. In the same chapter he says that a subject
who deliberately denies the authority of the commonwealth ceases to be a
subject and is dealt with by right of war, and then draws a conclusion that is
hard to read twice:

> And upon this ground it is, that also in Subjects, who deliberatly deny the
> Authority of the Common-wealth established, the vengeance is lawfully
> extended, not onely to the Fathers, but also to the third and fourth
> generation not yet in being, and consequently innocent of the fact, for
> which they are afflicted.

Punishment requires a law transgressed and a public judgement. What is done to
a declared enemy requires neither, which is why Hobbes has to say that
harm inflicted on him "fals not under the name of Punishment." Agamben's
homo sacer is the person in that position permanently: inside the territory,
outside the protection, bare life on which power acts directly. The wolf of
*De Cive* stops being a metaphor for what men are to each other and becomes a
description of what the ban produces.

### Esposito, and immunity

Roberto Esposito supplies the mechanism. A community, on his etymology, is a
set of obligations owed — a *munus*, a gift that binds. Immunity is exemption
from it. Hobbes's remedy for the danger that people pose one another is to cut
those obligations and route everything through a single point, so that subjects
are protected from each other precisely by no longer being bound to each other.
The commonwealth preserves life by negating the relations among the living, in
the way an inoculation protects by administering a controlled dose of what it
defends against.

This is why, on Esposito's account, the protection has no natural stopping
place. A politics that works by immunising can always find one more bond to
sever, and the pathology of the twentieth century was an immune response that
turned on the body it was defending.

### Negri, and the multitude

Antonio Negri, with Michael Hardt, takes the earlier moment.
[Chapter 16](/chapters/16-of-persons-authors-and-things-personated/) turns a
multitude into one person by having every member authorise a representative,
and for Hobbes this is the instant politics becomes possible at all. For Negri
it is the instant it is confiscated. The multitude — plural, unrepresented, not
reducible to one will — is not a defect to be cured by representation but a
political form in its own right, and Hobbes is the theorist who made its
disappearance look like a birth.

### Necropolitics

Achille Mbembe extended the argument where it was always most exposed. If
sovereignty's real content is the power to expose people to death, then the
place to observe it is not the European state but the colony — a space where
the sovereign operated outside law by design, where the inhabitants were
permanently in the position Hobbes reserves for enemies, and where none of the
protections that made subjects out of Englishmen ever applied. Hobbes's own
[example of the state of nature](/readings/post-colonial/) was America, which
makes the connection less of a leap than it looks.

### What it gets right

The passages are real, and they are not marginal — the right of punishing is
the foundation of the whole penal apparatus, and Hobbes puts it at the head of
the chapter because he knows the question is hard. Most readings of the book
skip it in a sentence. This one makes it the centre, and once you have seen it
you cannot read [Chapter 21](/chapters/21-of-the-liberty-of-subjects/) the same
way: the subject's liberty to resist a sovereign who comes to kill him is not a
loophole but the remains of a war that never formally ended.

There is also a straightforward sense in which Hobbes is a biopolitical thinker
before the word: the commonwealth exists to keep bodies alive, the sovereign's
title is protection and nothing else, and obligation lasts exactly as long as
the protection does. Foucault dates the administration of life to the
eighteenth century and treats Hobbes as its opposite number. The text makes
that periodisation harder than he allows.

### What it gets wrong

The complaints are worth stating, because this reading is easy to do badly.

It runs on a handful of passages. Chapter 28, the wolf of *De Cive*, the
frontispiece, and Chapter 16 will carry a great deal, but they are not the
book, and a reading that never has to account for
[Chapter 30](/chapters/30-of-the-office-of-the-soveraign-representative/) —
where the sovereign's duty is education, equal justice, the relief of the poor
and the encouragement of industry — has left out the half of *Leviathan* that
tells you what the power is for.

It also passes over the clause in the middle of its own key sentence.
*Excepting the limits set him by naturall Law*: the retained right is not
unbounded even in Hobbes's telling, and he spends
[Chapter 30](/chapters/30-of-the-office-of-the-soveraign-representative/)
saying what those limits require. A sovereign who kills the innocent is not
exercising an exception; he is failing at the one job that gave him the office.

And the genealogy is doing work the history may not support. Carl Schmitt is
the source of the exception, and his Hobbes was assembled inside the Third
Reich by a man with a professional interest in a state that could suspend its
own law. That the argument passed through him does not make it wrong. It does
mean that when this reading finds the camp latent in the covenant, it is
finding something a Nazi jurist put there, and the sentence in Chapter 28 is
what allows the rest of us to check.
