There is no avoiding the fact that this book was read as a manual for the
total state in the decade when total states were being built, and that one of
its best readers was a jurist of the Nazi party.

### Schmitt's failure

Carl Schmitt's argument in 1938 is not the one people expect. He admires the
sovereign, but reads the book as a defeat, and locates the defect in
[Chapter 37](/chapters/37-of-miracles-and-their-use/) and its neighbours: when
Hobbes concedes that a subject commanded to profess a faith he does not hold
may comply outwardly and believe as he likes inwardly, he has admitted a
distinction between public act and private conviction. Through that crack,
Schmitt says, came the liberal individual, the private sphere, and eventually
the dissolution of the state itself. The mortal god was killed by a
reservation its own maker wrote in.

### Behemoth answers Leviathan

The other German reading was written in exile. Franz Neumann called his study
of the Nazi regime *Behemoth*, after
[Hobbes's history of the civil war](/works/behemoth/), and the choice was the
argument: a regime of competing party, army, bureaucracy and industry, with no
rational law and no single authority, is not a Leviathan at all but the
condition Leviathan exists to prevent. On that reading Hobbes is not the
ancestor of the total state but its clearest critic.

Leo Strauss had set the terms of that German argument a few years earlier,
first in his notes on Schmitt's *Concept of the Political* and then in his own
book on Hobbes, where the question is what exactly the modern state was
founded on if not on a view of the good.

Hannah Arendt indicted him differently, as the philosopher of a class whose
logic of endless accumulation would not stop at any border.

### What the text supports

The absolutist case is real and on the page: the rights of
[Chapter 18](/chapters/18-of-the-rights-of-soveraignes-by-institution/) are
indivisible and unaccountable, the sovereign judges what doctrines may be
taught, and there is no lawful resistance.

The case against is equally textual. Belief cannot be commanded, only
profession. The subject's [liberty](/concepts/liberty/) includes an
inalienable right to defend his own life, and even to refuse a command to kill
himself or to incriminate himself. And the whole obligation
[lasts exactly as long as the power to protect](/chapters/21-of-the-liberty-of-subjects/).
A state that stops protecting has no claim, which is not a doctrine any
twentieth century regime would have wanted taught.

Michael Oakeshott's summary is the useful one: Hobbes is a philosopher of
authority, and authority in his sense is something individuals confer and
nothing else. That is a different animal from a state that claims to make the
individuals.
