Read Part I as physics and the book changes character. Sense is motion in the
organs; [imagination](/concepts/imagination/) is decaying sense;
[endeavour](/concepts/endeavour/) is motion too small to see; the passions are
endeavours toward or away; and deliberation is a sequence of them, of which
the last is called the [will](/concepts/will/). Nothing immaterial is
introduced at any point, and Hobbes is explicit in
[Chapter 34](/chapters/34-of-the-signification-of-spirit-angel-and-inspiration-in-the-books-of-holy-scripture/)
that an incorporeal substance is two words that destroy each other.

### What his contemporaries saw

They saw atheism, and they were not being stupid. If the world is body, then
[spirit](/concepts/spirit/) is either a thin body or a figure of speech, the
soul does not naturally survive, and God — whom Hobbes never denies — becomes
extremely hard to describe. Ralph Cudworth wrote a thousand pages against the
position. Samuel Mintz's survey of the reaction found that the materialism,
more than the politics, is what the English answerers actually attacked, and
[Chapter 46](/chapters/46-of-darknesse-from-vain-philosophy-and-fabulous-traditions/)
tells you why they were provoked: the schools' whole apparatus of essences and
separated forms is dismissed as names that name nothing.

Pierre Gassendi was doing much the same work in Paris in the same years, and
stopped just short of the same conclusion: atoms and void, sense as motion,
the Aristotelian forms dismissed — and an immaterial soul kept, because he was
a priest and would not give it up.

### What the materialists claimed

Engels, writing with Marx, put Hobbes in a line running from Bacon: English
materialism's first systematiser, the man who made the doctrine consistent by
making it unfriendly to theology. That genealogy is why Hobbes appears in
Soviet histories of philosophy as a progressive figure whose politics had to
be excused.

Cees Leijenhorst complicates the story from the other end: much of Hobbes's
physics is assembled out of the late scholastic vocabulary he claims to be
demolishing, so the break with Aristotle is less clean than Part IV pretends.

Thomas Spragens makes the larger version of that point. What Hobbes took from
Aristotle is not a doctrine but a shape: settle first what the world is made
of and how it moves, and let the account of men and cities follow from that.
Every answer is replaced and the order of the questions is kept, so a
cosmology of bodies that never come to rest delivers, quite properly, men who
never do either.

### Why it still matters

Because the position is recognisable. A world of bodies in motion, mental
states identified with motions in an organ, no immaterial residue, and an
account of reasoning as computation on names — that is close enough to modern
physicalism for the resemblance to be worth arguing about, and it is the
substance of the [claim on him](/ancestor/) made by philosophy of mind and by
artificial intelligence. He got there first, with almost no evidence, and was
hated for it.
