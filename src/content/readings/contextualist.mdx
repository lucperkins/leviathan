Quentin Skinner's objection to the way political theory was being taught in
the 1960s was simple and destructive: you cannot understand a text by asking
what it says about perennial questions, because the author was not answering
perennial questions. He was answering somebody, in a language, about something
that mattered that year. Recover the argument he was making and the moves he
was making in it, or you will read your own preoccupations back out of him.

### What it does to this book

A great deal. *Leviathan* was printed in 1651, in the middle of the
[Engagement controversy](/themes/the-english-civil-war/), when Englishmen were
being required to promise loyalty to a Commonwealth without king or Lords, and
the question of when a conquered subject owes obedience was the most practical
question in the country. The
[Review and Conclusion](/chapters/a-review-and-conclusion/) answers it
directly, which is why the royalists at the exiled court read the book as a
case for submitting to Cromwell and Jeffrey Collins has argued that they were
right.

Johann Sommerville's contribution is the deflationary one: much of what looks
like Hobbes's singular doctrine was common property in the debates of the
1640s, and the singularity is in the arrangement rather than the parts.
Richard Tuck put him inside the natural rights tradition; Noel Malcolm
established what the manuscripts and the printing actually show; J. G. A.
Pocock insisted that a reader who skips Parts III and IV is not reading the
book Hobbes wrote.

### The method's own targets

Contextualism was formed in opposition, and it is worth knowing to whom. It
denies that a text has a hidden doctrine recoverable by careful reading, which
is the [Straussian](/readings/straussian/) claim. It denies that a text can be
explained by the class position of its author, which is the
[marxist](/readings/marxist/) one. And it denies that the history of
philosophy is a conversation between contemporaries who happen to be dead.

### What it costs

Its own critics say the method makes the past inert: if every text is a move
in a vanished game, why read it at all? The Cambridge answer is that
recovering what somebody once argued shows that our own arrangements were
chosen rather than given — which is, as it happens, an argument Hobbes would
have understood, since the [commonwealth](/concepts/commonwealth/) is
[made rather than grown](/themes/artifice/).
