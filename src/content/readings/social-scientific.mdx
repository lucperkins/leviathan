Sociology inherited a question from Hobbes before it had a name for itself.
Talcott Parsons called it the Hobbesian problem of order: if people pursue
their own ends by whatever means work, why is there any society at all rather
than a war? Parsons thought Hobbes had posed it perfectly and answered it
badly, and the whole of his own theory is an attempt to do better.

### The problem, formally

[Chapter 13](/chapters/13-of-the-naturall-condition-of-mankind/) has the
structure of a collective action problem: everyone prefers peace to war,
nobody can afford to disarm first, and the man who keeps faith while others
break it makes himself a prey. The Foole of
[Chapter 15](/chapters/15-of-other-lawes-of-nature/) is the free rider,
and Hobbes's reply to him is an argument about repeated play — a man known for
breaking covenants cannot be received into any society except by an error he
cannot count on.

J. W. N. Watkins made Hobbes a system to be inspected;
[David Gauthier](/further-reading/#gauthier-logic) reconstructed the argument
as formal reasoning; Jean Hampton and Gregory Kavka then rebuilt it as
decision theory, Hampton to show that the sovereign cannot be created by the
very contract he is needed to enforce, Kavka to see what could be salvaged.

### The answers Hobbes did not give

The interesting turn is that the formal tools mostly undermine his conclusion.
Michael Taylor showed that in a repeated game conditional cooperation is
possible without an enforcer, so the sword is not the only exit.
Robert Axelrod showed the same thing by tournament. Elinor Ostrom went and
looked: irrigation systems, fisheries and alpine pastures governed for
centuries by their users, with rules and monitoring and no Leviathan.

Against that, Steven Pinker has argued from long-run homicide statistics that
states do in fact pacify, which is Hobbes's empirical claim rather than his
logical one, and it fares better.

### Why the reading survives

Because Hobbes wrote the problem down cleanly enough to be formalised, and
because he was explicit that his own answer was a piece of engineering rather
than a moral truth. That is what makes him usable by people who reject
almost everything he believed — and it is the strongest of the
[claims made on him](/ancestor/) by later disciplines.
