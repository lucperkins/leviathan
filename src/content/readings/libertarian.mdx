The libertarian interest in Hobbes is in the first half of his argument and
not the second. What is attractive is the starting point: individuals who owe
each other nothing, no natural authority anywhere, no common good given in
advance, and a state that has to be justified from scratch by what it does for
the people in it. Almost nobody before him had put the burden of proof that
way round.

### The derivation without the sovereign

Robert Nozick's minimal state is the clearest case. He runs the same problem —
what happens among people with rights and no common power — and gets a state
by an invisible hand rather than a covenant, then stops at protection. James
Buchanan takes the Hobbesian framing and makes it constitutional: the rules of
the game are chosen by the players in advance, and enforcement is the hard
part rather than an afterthought. David Gauthier takes the psychology and
tries to get a morality out of it. Jan Narveson takes the contractarianism and
gets rights.

What each of them wants is the [right of nature](/concepts/right-of-nature/)
without the surrender in
[Chapter 17](/chapters/17-of-the-causes-generation-and-definition-of-a-common-wealth/).

### Why Hobbes refuses to be one

Because his sovereign is not a nightwatchman. He is judge of doctrine, of
property, of what may be taught in the universities, of which religion is
publicly professed; there is no right against him except self-defence; and
[liberty](/concepts/liberty/) is whatever the law happens not to mention.
[Chapter 30](/chapters/30-of-the-office-of-the-soveraign-representative/) even
gives him public charity and the prevention of idleness. Murray Rothbard was
right that this is not a libertarian book.

### The honest version of the claim

Not that Hobbes was a libertarian, but that he built the machine libertarians
use. Start from individuals, derive authority from agreement, justify the
state by protection alone, and refuse to appeal to a natural hierarchy or a
highest good — that is his method, and it is neutral about how much state
comes out of it. Hobbes ran it and got a mortal god. Nozick ran it and got a
nightwatchman. Which of them made the arithmetic error is still the argument.
