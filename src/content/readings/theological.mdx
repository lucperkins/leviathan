The first thing to say is how much of it there is. Parts III and IV are longer
than Parts I and II together, and a reader who stops at the
[commonwealth](/concepts/commonwealth/) has read less than half the book.
Hobbes's contemporaries did not stop there, which is why they called him an
atheist rather than a tyrant.

### The two cases

A. P. Martinich's is that Hobbes meant it. On this reading he is a Christian
working in a recognisable Protestant tradition — scripture over tradition,
salvation by faith in one article, the church subordinate to the magistrate —
whose God is unusual because Hobbes will not allow attributes that pretend to
describe an incomprehensible nature. The apparent demolitions of
[spirit](/concepts/spirit/), [miracle](/concepts/miracle/) and
[prophecy](/concepts/prophet/) are then reforms rather than attacks: they
remove the parts of religion that had been used to raise armies.

Edwin Curley's is that the professions are cover. Hobbes defines terms until
almost nothing supernatural is left, ends prophecy and miracles with the
apostles, makes the soul mortal until the resurrection, hands every disputed
question to the sovereign, and does it all while insisting he is orthodox.
Curley reads this as an eighteenth century argument arriving early and in
disguise — and points at Hobbes's own remark that he dared not write so
boldly as he might have.

### The evidence both sides use

The same chapters. [Chapter 12](/chapters/12-of-religion/) derives religion
from fear and ignorance of causes and then distinguishes the natural seed from
true religion — is that a pious qualification or a fig leaf?
[Chapter 43](/chapters/43-of-what-is-necessary-for-a-mans-reception-into-the-kingdome-of-heaven/)
reduces the necessary faith to one article, that Jesus is the Christ, which is
either radical simplification in a good Protestant cause or the smallest
possible payment. [Chapter 44](/chapters/44-of-spirituall-darknesse-from-misinterpretation-of-scripture/)
attacks purgatory, exorcism and the natural immortality of the soul with a
thoroughness that is either reforming zeal or something else.

### Why it will not settle

Pocock made the eschatology unavoidable; Patricia Springborg the ecclesiastical
history; Jeffrey Collins the politics of the Erastian case. What none of them
can supply is access to what Hobbes believed, and the text was written by a man
who had watched people be prosecuted for their answers. The dispute is set out
on the page about [his faith](/hobbes/faith/), and it has not moved much in
three centuries.
