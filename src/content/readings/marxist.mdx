Marx himself read Hobbes as a materialist first and a political theorist
second, and the sentence he took from him is the one about
[worth](/concepts/honour/): a man's value is his price, so much as would be
given for the use of his power, and therefore not absolute.

### Possessive individualism

The book is C. B. Macpherson's
[*The Political Theory of Possessive Individualism: Hobbes to Locke*](/further-reading/#macpherson-possessive-individualism),
and its claim is precise. The state of nature is not a picture of men as such;
it is a picture of men whose relations are already those of a
possessive market society — where labour is alienable, where every man's worth
is set by others' need of him, and where security means securing one's means.
The restless desire of power after power in
[Chapter 11](/chapters/11-of-the-difference-of-manners/) is then not a
psychological universal but a description of competitors who cannot stop
competing.

The textual support is real. Hobbes says in
[Chapter 24](/chapters/24-of-the-nutrition-and-procreation-of-a-common-wealth/)
that a man's labour is a commodity exchangeable for benefit and that money is
the blood of the commonwealth; property exists because the sovereign
established it; and the
[passions that incline men to peace](/themes/commodious-living/) include the
desire for the things necessary to commodious living and the hope of getting
them by industry.

### The objections

Macpherson has been attacked from every direction. Keith Thomas argued that
Hobbes's own social world was aristocratic rather than bourgeois.
[Quentin Skinner](/further-reading/#skinner-reason-and-rhetoric) and the
contextualists object to the method: what matters is the argument Hobbes was
making in the debates he was actually in, not a model imputed to him from
three centuries away. Others deny that England in 1651 was a possessive market
society at all.

### After Macpherson

The tradition did not stop. Ellen Meiksins Wood put Hobbes inside a social
history of political thought without the machinery that got Macpherson into
trouble. Christopher Hill supplied the other half of the picture, the
radicalism of the 1640s that the sovereign was designed to end. Domenico
Losurdo turned the counter-history of liberalism into a genre.

Whatever one makes of the model, Macpherson asked the sharpest question anyone
has put to [Chapter 13](/chapters/13-of-the-naturall-condition-of-mankind/):
whose nature is this?
