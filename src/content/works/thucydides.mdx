He published nothing of his own until he was past fifty, and the first thing
he published at all was somebody else's book. The *Eight Bookes of the
Peloponnesian Warre* appeared in 1629, the first English Thucydides taken
straight from the Greek rather than through a French or Latin intermediary,
and Hobbes had been working at it privately for years before he let it out.

### Why Thucydides

The preface says what he was doing. Thucydides is praised as the most
politic historiographer that ever writ, and the reason given is not literary:
he shows what popular assemblies actually do, as against what orators say
they do. The Athenians debate, are flattered, change their minds, and lose
their empire; the whole of it is legible without a single moral drawn, because
the narrative does the work. Hobbes thought that a reader who followed the
Sicilian expedition would need no further argument against democracy.

Eleven years before the Long Parliament, this is already the shape of his
politics. What he objected to in the classical education of English gentlemen
was that they read Greek and Latin authors and came away in love with
tyrannicide; the counter-example he offered was a Greek author who had watched
a democracy destroy itself and written it down. The complaint recurs, still
angry, in [Chapter 29](/chapters/29-of-those-things-that-weaken-or-tend-to-the-dissolution-of-a-common-wealth/),
where reading the ancients is listed among the diseases of a commonwealth.

### What it does that *Leviathan* does not

Nothing in it is demonstrated. The translation belongs to the twenty years
before geometry, when he was still a humanist with a good ear rather than a
philosopher with a method, and it works by showing rather than proving. What
survives into the later work is the judgment: that men in assemblies are moved
by fear, honour, and interest, and that a state at war with itself will find
reasons afterwards.

He returned to translation at the very end, with the [Homer](/works/homer/),
so the working life is bracketed by two Greeks and the philosophy sits in the
middle.
