In the spring of 1640, with the Short Parliament quarrelling with the King
over exactly the question he had been thinking about, Hobbes finished a short
treatise and let it circulate in manuscript. He did not print it. Copies went
round London anyway, his authorship was known, and by the autumn he had
decided that England was not safe for him and left for France, afterwards
observing with some pride that he was the first of all that fled.

### What is in it

Two parts, and the division is the one he kept: human nature first, then the
body politic. Sense and imagination are explained by motion; the passions
follow; men are found to be roughly equal and therefore mutually afraid; the
condition of nature is a state of war; reason finds articles of peace; and a
covenant of all with all erects a sovereign whose power cannot be divided or
resisted. A reader who knows
[Leviathan](/chapters/17-of-the-causes-generation-and-definition-of-a-common-wealth/)
will recognise every step.

### What is missing

Two things, and they are what make *Leviathan* the book people argue about.
There is no theory of [personation](/concepts/person/): the sovereign is
authorised, but the machinery of representation that turns a multitude into
one person had not yet been invented, and without it the commonwealth is
harder to describe than to assert. And there is no Part III and IV. The
scriptural half, which is more than half of *Leviathan*, is barely present,
so the doctrine that made him notorious is missing from the book that states
the doctrine first.

### Its afterlife

He never printed it himself. In 1650 a publisher issued it in two halves,
*Human Nature* and *De Corpore Politico* (*On the Body Politic*), without
asking him, at the moment
*Leviathan* was going through the press. [Ferdinand Tönnies](/hobbes/impact/)
reunited the parts from Hobbes's own manuscripts in 1889, which is why it took
two hundred and fifty years for anyone to read it as the single book it was
written as.
