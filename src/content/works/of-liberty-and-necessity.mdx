In Paris in 1645 the Marquess of Newcastle got Hobbes and John Bramhall,
the exiled Bishop of Derry, to argue about free will in front of him, and
afterwards asked each to write his case down. Hobbes wrote his and asked that
it stay private. Nine years later a young admirer printed it anyway, with a
preface calling it a blow against the clergy, and Bramhall — who had kept his
side of the bargain — was understandably furious.

### The argument

Liberty is the absence of external impediments to motion. A man is free when
nothing stops him doing what he wills; he is not free with respect to the
willing, because the will is itself the last appetite in deliberating, and
appetites are caused. Both halves are needed. Deny the first and you make
liberty a mystery; deny the second and you make the will a thing that moves
without being moved, which is as unintelligible as an incorporeal substance.
So a man may be free and necessitated at once, and there is no contradiction:
the water in a channel descends freely and necessarily both.

The same doctrine is stated flatly in
[Chapter 21](/chapters/21-of-the-liberty-of-subjects/), where fear and liberty
are said to be consistent, and where a man who throws his goods overboard in a
storm does it willingly. What is a two-line assertion there is the whole
subject here.

### Why it went on so long

Bramhall replied in 1655; Hobbes answered at book length in 1656 with *The
Questions Concerning Liberty, Necessity, and Chance*, printing his opponent's
text section by section and answering each in turn, which is the method he had
already used on [Bellarmine](/interlocutors/bellarmine/). Bramhall came back
in 1658 with *The Catching of Leviathan*. Neither man conceded anything.

The stake was not academic. If the will is caused, then praise, blame, sin,
and punishment need reinterpreting, and Hobbes reinterprets them: punishment
looks forward to deterrence rather than back to desert, which is exactly the
[definition in Chapter 28](/chapters/28-of-punishments-and-rewards/). Bramhall
saw that clearly, and thought it destroyed morality. The argument between them
is still the argument.
