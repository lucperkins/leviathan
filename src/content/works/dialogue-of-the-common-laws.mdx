Late, unpublished in his lifetime, and in the form of a conversation between
a Philosopher and a Student of the Common Laws. The Student is given real
arguments and the Philosopher wins, but not by shouting: it is the most
patient thing Hobbes wrote after *Leviathan*, and the least angry.

### The quarrel with Coke

[Sir Edward Coke](/chapters/26-of-civill-lawes/) had held that the common law
is an artificial reason, refined by the study and experience of many
generations of judges, and therefore wiser than the natural reason of any one
man — including the King's. Hobbes takes the claim apart. Long study perfects
a man's knowledge of what has been decided; it does not turn a custom into an
obligation. What makes any rule law is that the sovereign commands it, and a
custom is law only because the sovereign, by not abolishing it, commands it
still. Judges are the sovereign's ministers, and their reason binds nobody in
its own right.

That is the doctrine of
[Chapter 26](/chapters/26-of-civill-lawes/) argued out against the best
opposing case, and it explains a footnote-sized joke there: Hobbes calls
statutes made by kings before the Conquest customs, and customs still obeyed
laws, in order to show that the distinction Coke needs will not hold.

### What else is in it

More than the title suggests. There are sections on heresy — written while he
was reading up on whether he could be burned for it — on punishment, on
praemunire, on the courts, and a long stretch on crimes and their mitigation
that supplements
[Chapter 27](/chapters/27-of-crimes-excuses-and-extenuations/). Throughout, the
Philosopher concedes far more to the lawyers than the reputation would predict:
he wants the common law kept, and wants only its authority correctly described.

### Its position

It matters because it is the one place Hobbes argues with a serious English
institution on that institution's own ground. Everywhere else the opponents are
Aristotle, the schoolmen, or Rome. Here it is the Inns of Court, and the
question is the one the civil war had actually been fought over: whether there
is any law in England that the King did not make.
