[Erastus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Erastus) was a physician at
Heidelberg who found himself in an argument about who may bar a sinner from
communion. His answer, written in 1568 and printed after his death, was that
nobody may: excommunication has no warrant in scripture, the church's
authority is to teach and warn, and every punishment belongs to the
magistrate. The word
[Erastianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erastianism) has since travelled a
long way from him, but it names the thing Hobbes argues for.

### The name

He was born Thomas Lüber, at Baden in 1524, and *Lüber* is German for beloved.
Grecising a name was ordinary among learned men of his generation — Schwarzerdt
became Melanchthon, Hausschein became Oecolampadius — and *Erastus* means the
same thing in Greek that Lüber means in German.

Half a century earlier a Dutchman had done it in both learned languages at
once: Gerrit Gerritszoon of Rotterdam became
[Desiderius Erasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus), desired in Latin
and beloved in Greek. Two beloveds, a country and a generation apart, and
readers have slid between them ever since. Only one of the two has a doctrine
of church and magistrate named after him.

### Where they agree

That coercion is indivisible and civil. In
[Chapter 42](/chapters/42-of-power-ecclesiasticall/) Hobbes takes apart the
claim that pastors hold a power of the keys in their own right: their
commission is to teach, and teaching persuades rather than compels; where
excommunication has any effect on a man's goods, liberty or life, that effect
is the civil sovereign's doing and nobody else's. A king excommunicated is
simply a king.

The reasoning is the same in both: a church is not a commonwealth, and a
society without a sword cannot punish.

### Where they part

In reach. Erastus argues inside a Christian commonwealth, from scripture and
the practice of the primitive church, and is not proposing a theory of the
state; he takes the magistrate's authority as given and asks only what the
consistory may add to it. Hobbes derives the magistrate's authority from a
covenant, makes him the sole judge of what may be publicly taught, and treats
the whole apparatus of a separate spiritual power as a
[disease](/themes/diseases-of-the-commonwealth/).

### Did Hobbes read him?

He did not need to. Erastus's book was printed in London in 1589 and was
being fought over at the Westminster Assembly in the 1640s, where
[John Selden](/kindred/john-selden/) argued the Erastian side while Hobbes was
in Paris writing. The position was the live English controversy of the decade
in which *Leviathan* was drafted.
