[Gassendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gassendi) was a Catholic priest, an astronomer, and the
man who spent his life making atomism respectable again by detaching it from
Epicurus's atheism. Hobbes met him in
[Mersenne's circle](/hobbes/contemporaries/) in the 1640s and the two got on,
which by Hobbes's standards is remarkable.

### Where they agree

On almost the whole programme of Part I. Sense is caused by motion in the
organs, not by species flying off objects. Qualities are in the perceiver
rather than the thing. Explanation means showing how bodies in motion produce
the effect, and the Aristotelian apparatus of forms, essences and final causes
explains nothing — the charge Hobbes lays out in
[Chapter 46](/chapters/46-of-darknesse-from-vain-philosophy-and-fabulous-traditions/).
Both wrote objections to Descartes's *Meditations* in the same volume, and
both objected in the same direction: that the thinking self does not get you
out of the body.

Gassendi's revival is also how the seventeenth century read
[Epicurus](/kindred/epicurus-and-lucretius/), whose account of justice as a
compact stands behind Hobbes's.

### Where they part

At exactly the point a priest would stop. Gassendi keeps an immaterial,
immortal rational soul, keeps free will, and keeps God's providence
operating in the world; the atoms are created and directed. Hobbes will not
allow an incorporeal substance anywhere, and the disagreement is not a detail
but the difference between a Christianised Epicureanism and a thoroughgoing
materialism.

### Did Hobbes read him?

They knew each other, corresponded through Mersenne, and Gassendi is reported
to have admired *De Cive*. He is on the list of
[surprising omissions](/thinkers/#surprising-omissions) all the same: the ally
he never names.
